The AI Brain: Sensor Fusion & Real-Time Control

The car has no pilot. An AI control unit reads speed, steering angle, brake state and autonomous-driving sensors to build a live picture of the vehicle and its surroundings.

Sensor Fusion

One unified model of the car's state, built from many inputs. The AI resolves conflicting signals and decides what the vehicle is actually doing mid-air.

Split-Second Decisions

Humans can't manage G-forces and attitude drift in real time. The AI decides when to fire and how hard — thousands of times faster.

Autopilot for the Flight Phase

Think of it as an autopilot for the jump: it stabilizes the car, holds attitude, and hands control back to the driver for landing.

The Muscles: Pneumatics, Actuators & Thrusters

The AI's decisions are useless without hardware that can execute them precisely.

Cold-Gas Thrusters (Pneumatics)

No combustion. Compressed air from carbon-fibre tanks at up to 10,000 PSI is released through nozzles. Pure pneumatics — repeatable and controllable.

Electric Micro-Actuators

Valve control comes from electric actuators (some derived from Tesla's Optimus program), enabling millisecond-level changes in thrust force and direction.

Underbody Suction Fans

High-power electric turbines create a vacuum beneath the car, generating up to 2G of downforce — enough to stick it to a surface instead of falling off.

Why It Doesn't Fall: The Suction Fan System

The upside-down drive is a separate trick from the thrusters. It's not ballistic — it's active aerodynamic adhesion. The car literally vacuums itself to the surface, creating an artificial gravity stronger than Earth's.

Air Extraction

Fans underneath the chassis pull air out of the gap between the car and the road, then expel it out the back. The gap becomes a low-pressure zone.

Pressure Difference

Normal atmospheric pressure above, near-vacuum below. The car is physically pushed down onto the surface — and stays there even when inverted.

Up to 2G of Downforce

The system generates roughly double the car's weight in downforce — over 4,700 kg at peak — enough to overcome gravity entirely.

The Nervous System: IoT & Sensor Networks

The car is a connected machine. Its sensors aren't just for driving — they're the AI's eyes during the jump and the suction system's feedback loop.

Always-On Sensor Array

Speed, steering, brake and autonomy sensors stream continuously into the controller. No data, no stable hover.

Drive-by-Wire

Steer-by-wire and brake-by-wire give the AI clean electronic signals to command — no mechanical lag between decision and action.

Mode Handoffs

The hard part isn't flying — it's the seamless switch between driving, suction, and thrust. That orchestration is software.

How Efficient Is AI Here? It's the Enabling Layer.

AI isn't a nice-to-have in this system. Without it, a two-ton car cannot hold attitude mid-air or modulate suction for inverted driving.

Microsecond Precision

AI reads sensors and issues commands in fractions of a second — far beyond human reaction time.

System Orchestration

AI is the glue between pneumatics, suction fans and aerodynamics — deciding which subsystem acts, and when.

Gas Efficiency

Aerospace research shows AI controllers can hit precise pressure control with under 0.1 bar steady-state error — squeezing more control from every litre of air.

The Recap: What Was Actually Demonstrated

Two distinct systems — one for spectacle, one for grip — both orchestrated by software.

01

Ramp launch, mid-air barrel roll, landing on all four wheels — thrusters at work.

02

Jumping over a line of parked Teslas with a “kickflip”-style manoeuvre.

03

Hovering in place and rotating — the clearest proof of active stabilization.

04

Driving fully inverted without falling — suction fans generating up to 2G of adhesion.

Brief Hops, Not Flight

The thrusters can't lift a ~2-ton car for long. What you see is a brief, low hop or hover — not sustained flight.

Not Road-Legal

Tesla's own configurator states the thruster package is “not approved for public road use”.
